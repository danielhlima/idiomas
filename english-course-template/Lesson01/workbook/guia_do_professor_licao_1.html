Teacher Guide Lesson 1 - Nice to Meet You
Slide 1 of 28

Cover

On the classroom screen: English Course, Lesson 1, Introductions.

Objective

Set the scene for the class: first lesson, first meeting, international and professional context.

How to Teach

  • Greet the class in English: "Good morning" or "Good evening".
  • Show the image and ask what they notice before explaining any content.
  • Explain that the whole lesson simulates a first day at an international company.

Suggested Script

"Today we start Lesson 1. The situation is simple: first day at an international company. You will meet people, say your name, your job and where you are from."

Note

Do not introduce grammar yet. The initial goal is confidence and context, not accuracy.

Time: 2 minMode: whole class

Objective

How to Teach

Slide 3 of 28

Warm-up

On the classroom screen: an image of people meeting and observation questions.

Objective

Activate prior knowledge and get students speaking even with limited English.

How to Teach

  1. Give students 30 seconds to observe the image silently.
  2. Ask the questions one by one.
  3. Accept short answers: "office", "six people", "happy", "meeting".
  4. Reformulate answers in correct English without interrupting too much.

Light Correction

Student: "They in office." Teacher: "Good. They are in an office."

Note

This slide says there are no right or wrong answers. Keep the atmosphere safe.

Time: 5 minMode: whole class

Greetings

On the classroom screen: hello, goodbye and useful expressions.

Objective

Present basic communicative language for greetings and farewells.

How to Teach

  • Model each expression with natural intonation.
  • Ask for group repetition and then pair repetition.
  • Highlight context differences: time of day, first meeting, leaving.

Micro-Practice

Say situations and ask for quick responses: "8 a.m.", "first meeting", "leaving work".

Note

Do not explain pronunciation technically. Use modeling and short repetition.

Time: 6 minMode: repetition + pairs
Slide 5 of 28

Choose the Best Expression

On the classroom screen: situations with three options each.

Objective

Consolidate the choice of appropriate expressions in workplace contexts.

How to Teach

  • Read the situation, give students 5 seconds and ask for a finger vote: 1, 2 or 3.
  • Then ask one student to say the full expression.
  • Repeat the correct expression with the class.

Pace

Keep it quick. This is an initial fluency exercise, not a long discussion.

Note

"Good night" is not a common greeting in this context; if it comes up, explain that it is usually a farewell before going to sleep.

Time: 4 minMode: whole class

Introducing Yourself

On the classroom screen: expressions for name, job, company and origin.

Objective

Give students a ready-to-use model for a professional self-introduction.

How to Teach

  • Read the cards in sequence as if it were a real introduction.
  • Ask for line-by-line repetition.
  • Replace Daniel with your own name to model personalization.

Quick Practice

Ask students to choose 3 sentences to say about themselves. Do not require all of them yet.

Note

Do not explain the verb to be yet. Use the sentences as communicative chunks.

Slide 7 of 28

Introducing Yourself and Someone

On the classroom screen: complete introduction image and prompts for introducing yourself and a colleague.

Objective

Bring two simple skills together in one oral practice: talking about yourself and introducing another person.

How to Teach

  1. Model it yourself: "Hello, I'm Daniel. I'm a teacher. I'm from Brazil."
  2. Then point to a volunteer and model: "This is Ana. She's a colleague. She's from Brazil."
  3. Put students in pairs. Each student gives their own introduction first.
  4. Then join two pairs. Each student introduces their partner to the other pair.

Tablet Model

"First say: I'm... Then say: This is... Use he for a man and she for a woman. Keep it short."

Note

Do not turn this into a grammar lesson. If there is a he/she error, simply reformulate correctly and keep fluency moving.

Time: 8 minMode: pairs + groups of 4
Slide 8 of 28

Jobs

On the classroom screen: six jobs plus example questions and answers with workplace prepositions.

Objective

Present essential job vocabulary and show the correct prepositions for common workplace answers.

How to Teach

  • Read each job and ask for repetition.
  • Ask who knows someone with that job.
  • Connect the words to sentences: "I'm a..." without explaining articles in detail.
  • Use the examples to highlight: work as + job, work at + company, in + department.

Quick Activity

Ask one question and point to the preposition: "Where do you work?" -> "I work at GlobalTech." Then students create one answer with as, at or in.

Note

Avoid long lists of extra jobs. Keep the focus on the lesson vocabulary and on the three useful patterns: as + job, at + company, in + department.

Time: 6 minMode: whole class
Slide 9 of 28

Countries & Nationalities

On the classroom screen: country -> nationality pairs, capitalization note and example questions.

Objective

Differentiate country and nationality for professional introductions and reinforce that nationalities use capital letters in English.

How to Teach

  • Model: "I'm from Brazil. I'm Brazilian."
  • Ask the class to repeat in pairs: country, nationality.
  • Ask quick prompts: "France?" The class answers "French".
  • Point to the examples and ask: "Country or nationality?" before students answer.

Key Point

Country answers "Where are you from?". Nationality answers "What's your nationality?". In English: Brazilian, British, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese and American start with capital letters.

Note

Do not use flags or stereotypes. Keep the focus linguistic.

Time: 6 minMode: quick drilling

Slide 13 of 28

Personal Pronouns

On the classroom screen: I, you, he, she, it, we, they table.

Objective

Show that English sentences usually need an explicit subject.

How to Teach

  • Use students' names and character names to replace them with pronouns.
  • Show the contrast: "Am a developer" is incorrect; "I am a developer" is correct.
  • Ask for examples: "Daniel" -> "he"; "Emma" -> "she"; "Daniel and Emma" -> "they".

Key Point

In Portuguese, we often omit the subject; in A1 English, we will always use the subject.

Note

Do not complicate "it" with exceptions. Use "ID badge", "office", "company".

Time: 8 minMode: explanation + check
Slide 14 of 28

The Verb to Be

On the classroom screen: am, is, are with subjects.

Objective

Teach the affirmative form of the verb to be for identity, job, nationality and location.

How to Teach

  1. Present it as a table with three families: I am; he/she/it is; you/we/they are.
  2. Say that the verb changes according to the person, but keep the explanation short.
  3. Do echo repetition with the class: "I am", "She is", "They are".
  4. Then complete identity sentences: "I am Daniel", "She is Emma", "They are colleagues".
  5. Close by returning to the professional context: name, job, nationality, company.

Check

Do call and response: you say "I", the class says "am"; you say "Emma", the class says "is"; you say "Daniel and Emma", the class says "are". Then ask for a complete sentence with each one.

Note

Do not introduce questions or negatives yet; they will come later.

Time: 8 minMode: explanation + drilling
Slide 15 of 28

Full Forms and Contractions

On the classroom screen: I am/I'm, you are/you're, etc.

Objective

Help students recognize full forms and contractions as equivalent.

How to Teach

  • Read the full form first, then the contraction.
  • Explain that contractions sound more natural in speech.
  • Ask for oral transformation: "I am" -> "I'm"; "They are" -> "They're".

Quick Practice

Show a full sentence and ask for the contracted version; then do the opposite.

Note

Do not treat contractions as too informal. In professional conversations, they are natural.

Time: 7 minMode: oral transformation
Slide 16 of 28

Building Affirmative Sentences

On the classroom screen: Subject + Verb to Be + Complement structure.

Objective

Organize the basic order of affirmative sentences in English.

How to Teach

  • Write in the air or on the board: Subject | be | complement.
  • Use examples from the screen and ask the class to identify each part.
  • Do oral sentence building: "Mia / project manager" -> "Mia is a project manager."

Check

Ask: "What is the subject?", "What is the verb?", "What is the complement?"

Note

Do not overload students with grammar terms. Use them as a tool, not as the goal.

Time: 7 minMode: guided analysis
Slide 17 of 28

Grammar Practice

On the classroom screen: button to open external affirmative grammar practice.

Objective

Practice pronouns, am/is/are, contractions and sentence structure.

How to Teach

  • Open the practice and solve the first items with the class.
  • Then let students try individually or in pairs.
  • When correcting, ask "Why am/is/are?" to reinforce the rule.

Strategy

If the class is tired, select key items and leave the rest as review.

Note

Do not move on to negatives until am/is/are feels reasonably stable.

Slide 18 of 28

The Verb to Be - Negative

On the classroom screen: Subject + be + not + complement structure.

Objective

Teach how to make information negative with am not, is not and are not.

How to Teach

  • Start with contrast: "I am a teacher" / "I am not a student".
  • Visually highlight that "not" comes after the verb to be.
  • Use examples with jobs and nationalities.

Check

Say an affirmative sentence and ask for the negative version.

Note

Do not introduce "don't" here. For this lesson, negatives are with the verb to be.

Time: 8 minMode: guided explanation
Slide 19 of 28

Negative Contractions

On the classroom screen: is not/isn't, are not/aren't, I'm not.

Objective

Show natural negative forms in speech and everyday writing.

How to Teach

  • Show that "am not" becomes "I'm not", not "amn't".
  • Do transformation: "She is not" -> "She isn't".
  • Ask for repetition with natural rhythm.

Quick Practice

The teacher says the full form; the class answers with the contraction.

Note

Accept both full forms and contractions in student answers.

Time: 6 minMode: drilling
Slide 20 of 28

Using Negative Sentences

On the classroom screen: using negatives to correct information.

Objective

Apply negatives in communicative situations: correcting job, country, location or identity.

How to Teach

  • Say incorrect information and ask for correction: "Emma is Brazilian."
  • Student answers: "No, she isn't. She's British."
  • Do it in pairs using cards/characters from the previous screen.

Student Activity

Ask each student to say one true sentence about themselves and one negative sentence.

"I'm Brazilian. I'm not Spanish." / "I'm a manager. I'm not an intern."

Note

If a student only says "No", encourage the full sentence, but accept it as an initial step. Keep the activity personal or fictional; do not force real company information.

Time: 8 minMode: oral correction
Slide 21 of 28

Grammar Practice

On the classroom screen: external practice with negatives.

Objective

Review am not, isn't, aren't and correction of information.

How to Teach

  • Explain that full forms and contractions can both be correct.
  • Ask students to read feedback carefully.
  • After a few items, ask them to explain their choice to a partner.

Check

If many students make mistakes, return to the previous slide for 2 minutes and redo examples.

Note

Reinforce: "He isn't" = "He is not"; both forms are valid.

Time: 10-15 minMode: external practice
Slide 22 of 28

Yes/No Questions and Short Answers

On the classroom screen: questions with am/is/are plus affirmative and negative short answers.

Objective

Teach question word order with the verb to be and help students answer with yes/no plus the correct verb.

How to Teach

  • Show the transformation: "You are a visitor" -> "Are you a visitor?"
  • Use a physical gesture: the verb moves to the front.
  • Then point to the short answers: "Yes, I am." / "No, I'm not."
  • Ask students to answer orally using the same verb they hear in the question.

Controlled Practice

Say: "Are you Brazilian?" Students answer: "Yes, I am." Then say: "Are you Spanish?" Students answer: "No, I'm not."

No vs. Not

Explain simply: no answers the question; not makes the verb negative. Example: "No, I'm not."

Time: 8 minMode: guided transformation
Slide 23 of 28

Personal and Work Questions

On the classroom screen: questions with what, where and which for workplace introductions.

Objective

Help students ask about name, job, workplace, department, country and nationality using common question words.

How to Teach

  • Point to what: use it for general information, such as name or job.
  • Point to where: use it for places, such as country or company.
  • Point to which: use it when choosing from a known group, such as a department.
  • Read the mini-dialogue once, then ask students to replace the answers with their own information.

Micro-Dialogue

A: What's your name? B: My name is Ana. A: What do you do? B: I'm an analyst. A: Which department are you in? B: I'm in Sales.

Note

Teach "What do you do?" and "Where do you work?" as complete workplace questions now. Avoid a long do/does explanation in Lesson 1.

Time: 6 minMode: mingling

Question Review

Objective

Bring all useful questions together before the final grammar and communication practice.

How to Teach

Grammar Practice

On the classroom screen: external practice with questions and short answers.

Objective

Consolidate word order in questions, short answers and personal questions.

How to Teach

Slide 26 of 28

Communication

On the classroom screen: final activity "Meet a New Colleague".

Objective

Use everything from the lesson in an oral activity with an interview and colleague introduction.

How to Teach

  1. Explain the situation: first day at an international company.
  2. Pairs: A interviews B; then they switch roles.
  3. Pairs join together: each student introduces their partner.
  4. Ask for short presentations to the class.

Teacher's Role

Circulate, note recurring errors and give general feedback at the end without interrupting speaking too much.

Note

Do not let students read everything. Encourage short notes and natural speech.

Time: 12-15 minMode: pairs -> groups of 4
Slide 27 of 28

Listening Challenge

On the classroom screen: button to open the listening challenge with five audio files.

Objective

Train listening comprehension focused on names, jobs, countries, companies and work information.

How to Teach

  • Explain that students do not need to understand every word.
  • Tell them to listen first for general meaning.
  • Then ask them to pay attention to specific details in the questions.
  • Release the transcript only after the first attempt.

Strategy

For a live class, do 1 or 2 audio files together and leave the rest for individual practice.

Note

Do not turn listening into translation. The focus is to locate information and confirm hypotheses.

Time: 10-20 minMode: guided listening
