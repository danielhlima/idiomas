Grammar

Singular and Plural Nouns

Singular = one thing.

One computer. One notebook.

Plural = two or more things.

For most nouns, add -s.

computer → computers

notebook → notebooks

For some nouns, add -es.

Use -es after -s, -x, -sh, and -ch.

bus → buses

box → boxes

watch → watches

Examples

Singular

  • computer
  • notebook
  • bus
  • box
  • watch

Plural

  • computers
  • notebooks
  • buses
  • boxes
  • watches

Remember

  • One computer.
  • Two computers.
  • One chair.
  • Three chairs.

Now let's practise!

Grammar

Imperatives and Let's

Imperatives

Use the base form of the verb.

We use imperatives to give instructions, directions, and simple commands.

Good to know

The imperative has the same form for one person or many people.

  • Look at the screen. (one person)
  • Look at the screen. (everyone)
  • Open the door. (one person)
  • Open the door. (everyone)
Let's...

Use Let's to make a suggestion or start an activity together.

Examples

Imperatives

  • Look at the screen.
  • Open the door.
  • Close the window.
  • Take your notebook.
  • Put the pens on the table.

Let's

  • Let's begin.
  • Let's continue.
  • Let's practise.
  • Let's check.

Remember

  • Listen.
  • Look.
  • Read.
  • Write.

These are all imperatives.
Now let's practise!

Transcript

Daniel: Thanks, Sophie! Wow, this office is beautiful. It's much bigger than your old one.

Sophie: Thank you! I'm really happy here. Everything is new, and it's much brighter.

Daniel: I like the furniture. The white desk looks modern, and the black chair looks very comfortable.

Sophie: Yes, and the computer is new too. It's on the desk, next to the phone.

Sophie: I use both screens every day. They're really useful for my work.

Daniel: I also like your blue notebook. Is it always on the desk?

Sophie: Not always. Today it's in my bag because I have an afternoon meeting.

Daniel: Where is your bag?

Sophie: It's under the desk, next to the grey printer.

Daniel: Oh, I didn't see the printer. It's quite small.

Sophie: Yes, but it's very useful. I use it almost every day.

Daniel: I can also see a green water bottle and a red coffee mug.

Daniel: Everything is very clean and well organized.

Sophie: I try to keep my desk tidy. It's easier to work that way.

Sophie: They're behind the monitor. I always leave them there.

Sophie: (Laughs.) That happens to me too sometimes.

Daniel: Is that a family photo?

Sophie: Yes, it is. It's in front of the monitor. I like having it on my desk.

Daniel: The office feels very comfortable and welcoming.

Sophie: Thank you! I really enjoy working here.

Daniel: I can understand why. It's a fantastic office!

Grammar

Prepositions of Place

We use prepositions to describe where people and objects are.

Prepositions show position.

  • on The phone is on the desk.
  • in The notebook is in the bag.
  • under The keys are under the chair.
  • next to The bag is next to the desk.
  • between The coffee mug is between the phone and the notebook.
  • behind The keys are behind the monitor.
  • in front of The photo is in front of the monitor.

New Vocabulary

  • printerThe printer is grey.
  • furnitureThe furniture is new.
  • photoThe photo is on the desk.
  • coffee mugThe coffee mug is red.
  • usefulThe printer is useful.
  • tidyThe desk is tidy.
  • brightThe office is bright.
  • welcomingThe office is welcoming.

Use prepositions, colours and adjectives to describe objects and places.

Lesson 2

Expressions to Remember

You don't need to understand all the grammar yet. Learn these expressions as they are. We will study them in future lessons.

Sometimes you will hear expressions that use grammar we haven't studied yet.

Don't worry. Learn these expressions as complete chunks.

But...

Use but to connect two different ideas.

  • I'm tired, but I'm happy.

Why...?

Use Why to ask for a reason.

  • Why are you nervous?
  • Why is the meeting important?

I don't...

Learn this expression as it is.

  • I don't know.
  • I don't want to make mistakes.

There is... / There are...

Learn these expressions as complete chunks.

  • There is a phone on the desk.
  • There are a lot of people in the meeting room.

I still...

Learn this expression as it is.

  • I still feel nervous.
  • I still have my notebook.

look + adjective

Use it to describe how someone appears.

  • You look relaxed.
  • You look tired.

I hope so.

This is a very common expression.

  • Your presentation is very good.
  • I hope so.

Good luck!

A common expression before an important event.

  • Good luck!
  • Good luck with your presentation!

Thank you very much. / You're welcome.

Useful expressions for everyday conversations.

  • Thank you very much.
  • You're welcome.

Don't translate every word. Try to understand the whole expression.

Little by little, these expressions will become natural.
